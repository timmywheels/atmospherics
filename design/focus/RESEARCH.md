# Sounds for focus: what the research supports

Notes behind the Focus mixes and the in-app "How we chose these" page (`FocusScienceView`).
Compiled October 2026. Evidence levels are our own plain-language reading, not a formal grading.
None of this is medical advice, and the app makes no medical claims.

## 1. Steady sound beats changing sound (strong)

- **Irrelevant sound / speech effect.** Background speech and lyrics impair serial recall and
  verbal tasks even when people try to ignore them; sounds that keep changing (ticking, typing,
  varied tones) disrupt more than steady ones. Decades of lab work; see the review in
  Perham & Currie (2014), *Applied Cognitive Psychology*.
- **Masking.** Steady broadband noise and water sounds mask nearby speech. Haapakangas et al.
  (2011, *Acta Acustica*) found a water sound the best masker in the lab for satisfaction and
  performance; a long-term open-plan office trial (Hongisto et al., 2017, *Frontiers in
  Psychology*, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5515102/) did not confirm water's edge
  over standard masking noise.
- **In the app:** Quiet Car (pink noise + stream), Steady Rain, Reading Room (the ticking
  clock was taken out). Late Draft (typing, clock) and Temple Garden (chimes, birdsong) moved to
  Relax, since their sounds keep changing.

## 2. White/pink noise for attention difficulties (some, for ADHD)

- Nigg, Bruton, Kozlowski, Johnstone & Karalunas (2024), "Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis:
  Do White Noise and Pink Noise Help With Attention in Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity
  Disorder?", *Journal of the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry* 63(8),
  778–788. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11283987/
  - 13 studies, N = 335: small benefit for ADHD or elevated symptoms (g = 0.249, 95% CI
    0.135–0.363); a small cost for non-ADHD comparison groups (g ≈ −0.21).
  - No studies of brown noise.
- Proposed mechanism: moderate brain arousal / stochastic resonance (Söderlund et al., 2007),
  plausible but not established.
- **In the app:** Restless Mind (pink + white), framed as "try it if your mind wanders".

## 3. Beta-rate amplitude modulation (early)

- Woods, Loui et al. (2024), "Rapid modulation in music supports attention in listeners with
  attentional difficulties", *Communications Biology*. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-024-07026-3
  - Music with added amplitude modulation; beta-range modulation helped sustained attention more
    for participants with more ADHD symptoms; greater attention-network activity (fMRI) and
    stimulus–brain coupling (EEG).
  - One paper; the authors work with Brain.fm, whose music was used.
- **In the app:** Beta Pulse (pink noise modulated at 16 Hz, depth 0.5, both ears in phase so it
  works on a speaker), with soft rain.

## 4. Binaural beats (mixed)

- Lane, Kasian, Owens & Marsh (1998), *Physiology & Behavior* 63, 249–252.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9423966/ — 16/24 Hz beats in pink noise gave better
  vigilance and mood than 1.5/4 Hz beats (n = 29; no silent control).
- Colzato, Barone, Sellaro & Hommel (2017), *Psychological Research* — 40 Hz beats reduced the
  global-precedence effect (more focused attention), n = 36.
- Garcia-Argibay, Santed & Reales (2019), meta-analysis, *Psychological Research*.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30073406/ — overall g = 0.45 across cognition, anxiety and
  pain; exposure before the task, and longer exposure, did better.
- Ingendoh, Posny & Heine (2023), *PLOS ONE*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10198548/
  — of 14 EEG studies, 5 supported entrainment, 8 contradicted it, 1 mixed.
- **In the app:** Beta Beats (192/208 Hz, a 16 Hz beat, in pink noise) and Gamma Beats
  (220/260 Hz, a 40 Hz beat, in brown noise), both marked "headphones" and "mixed evidence".

## 5. Moderate noise for creative work (some)

- Mehta, Zhu & Cheema (2012), "Is Noise Always Bad? Exploring the Effects of Ambient Noise on
  Creative Cognition", *Journal of Consumer Research* 39(4), 784–799.
  https://doi.org/10.1086/665048 — 70 dB ambient noise beat 50 dB on creative tasks; 85 dB hurt.
- **In the app:** Coffee Shop, labelled for creative work rather than detail work.

## 6. Nature sounds and attention restoration (some)

- Van Hedger et al. (2019), "Of cricket chirps and car horns", *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*.
  https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-018-1539-1 — nature soundscapes improved backward digit span /
  dual n-back performance relative to urban soundscapes.
- Buxton et al. (2021), *PNAS* — synthesis linking natural sounds to better health and affect
  and lower stress and annoyance; evidence on cognition specifically is thinner.
- **In the app:** Green Break (birdsong, stream, leaves), 10 minutes between focus blocks.
